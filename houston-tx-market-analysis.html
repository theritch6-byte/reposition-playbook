Houston’s Inner Loop scores 5.42 / 10 — Tier F (Pass). It is the most operationally capable, most regulatorily permissive, and most genuinely durable market screened for this exchange — and it carries the worst entry arithmetic of the eight. Inner Loop STR-grade product trades at a 14.6× price-to-revenue multiple (Heights 18.0×, Montrose 15.3×, EaDo 10.8× at best), against a ~6× threshold above which a 20% gross floor stops being arithmetically reachable. A 3BR earning the Inner Loop median of $36,000 justifies $180,000 at the 20% floor — $51,000 below the $231,000 1031 minimum. The compliant price window and the justified price window do not intersect at any transactable point.

Market context: At 5.42/10, Houston’s Inner Loop sits at the very bottom of the 5.4–6.2 band the framework reserves for a single-driver COVID-boom market that didn’t sustain — and far below event-driven and coastal anchors like Destin, FL (6.9–7.5, the Tier C/B border) or Luray, VA (~7.0, Tier C). The unusual signature is that Houston earns that low score from the opposite direction: its demand is genuinely primary-market durable and its operations bench is the deepest on the board, but a 2.1252% uncapped property tax and a 14.6× entry multiple overwhelm both.

What breaks it: All three buy-box lanes fail the 15% gross-ROI floor (8.87% / 8.31% / 7.71%), all three produce negative net cash flow at conservative revenue, and all three go DISTRESS in the bear case. Cap rate runs 3.3–5.6% against a 7.125% cost of debt — negative leverage.
Budget Fit — TIGHT
At $385,000, the math requires $75,033 in gross revenue to reach $30,000 net — above this market’s 3BR top-quartile ceiling of $52,000, and above even Houston’s top-decile listing at $69,684. No price point inside the $600,000 search ceiling closes this gap.

⚖ Regulatory & Legal
STR Legal Citywide

Ordinance 2025-322 (Ch. 28, Art. XXIII), adopted April 16, 2025, effective January 1, 2026. No ban, no moratorium, no cap on the number of STRs, no density or spacing limit, no owner-occupancy requirement, and no zoning. An LLC may hold the certificate. This is the most permissive big-city STR framework in Texas.

Ministerial Issuance

Sec. 28-732(a): the director shall approve a complete, conforming application. No neighbor-objection step, no public hearing, no notice to adjacent owners, no discretionary compatibility test, no lottery. Cost is $275/yr plus a $33.10 city administrative fee, online, single sitting, no inspection.

Deed Restrictions — TRIGGERED

Houston has no zoning, so recorded private covenants govern land use — and Houston is the only Texas city whose Legal Department sues to enforce private covenants by injunction, via a dedicated Deed Restriction Enforcement Team. Sec. 28-731(b)(10) requires a sworn owner acknowledgment that STR use violates no covenant, HOA rule, or minimum-occupancy-duration term; Sec. 28-733(a)(2) makes false application information an independent revocation ground. Heights and Montrose are HIGH risk; EaDo is lowest.

Certificate Void on Sale

Sec. 28-736(b): the certificate is non-transferable and goes void on sale. A 1031 buyer re-applies from scratch — budget 2–6 weeks of registration lag at closing, and do not assume a seller’s registered-and-operating status conveys.

17% Combined Lodging Tax

6% State of Texas + 7% City of Houston + 2% Harris County + 2% Harris County–Houston Sports Authority — well above the 12% screening threshold. Airbnb and Vrbo auto-remit all four layers under agreements with Houston First; every other channel, including direct booking, is owner-remitted.

Jurisdiction Trap

Bellaire and West University Place are incorporated islands entirely surrounded by Houston and both prohibit STRs in residential districts. Pearland banned new residential STRs on February 9, 2026; Sugar Land prohibits them in single-family zones. A Houston address on a listing sheet is not proof of Houston jurisdiction.

Flood Regulatory Overlay

Chapter 19 (effective Sept 1, 2018) requires new construction and substantial improvements anywhere in the 500-year floodplain to be elevated 2 feet above the 500-year elevation, with zero net fill. Harris County’s MAAPnext draft maps, released February 2026, expand the 100-year floodplain ~43% (~130 sq mi) and move ~65,000 properties from Zone X into Zone AE, finalizing around 2028.

No State Preemption

Texas cities may ban STRs outright — SB 451 (2017), HB 2665 (2023) and HB 5225 (2025) all died. Direction of travel is city-by-city, and around Houston it is anti-STR in the suburbs and pro-STR in the core. Enforcement in 2026 has been cooperative: 83% compliance, ~4,000 registered, delisting deferred to January 1, 2027.

2027 Property-Tax Cap Sunset

Texas gives investment property no appraisal-cap protection — the 10% homestead cap does not apply. The temporary SB 2 20% non-homestead circuit breaker sunsets December 31, 2026, and the next regular legislative session does not convene until January 2027. Model 2027+ at full market value, uncapped.

📈 Demand & Market Fundamentals
Texas Medical Center

60+ institutions, 21 hospitals, 106,000+ employees, 10,000 beds, ~180,000 daily visitors and $25B of annual economic impact — the largest medical complex on earth, anchored by the #1 US cancer center. It is the one year-round, Inner-Loop-concentrated driver, but it is MTR-shaped rather than nightly-STR-shaped, and a 250-room hotel plus a 300-unit residential tower break ground on campus in 2026 as direct competing supply.

Rodeo — 2.6M Attendees

The Houston Livestock Show & Rodeo ran March 2–22, 2026 and drew 2.6 million attendees. It is the single largest annual compression event and the reason March is the peak month.

Conventions & GRB

A $1.4B, 700,000 sq ft George R. Brown expansion opens May 2028, and the RNC comes to Houston in 2028. Houston First hit a new convention sales record in 2024. This driver is moderate now and materially stronger after 2028.

#1 US Population Growth

Houston metro led the entire nation with nearly 127,000 new residents in the 12 months to July 1, 2025 — 347 a day — on a 7.5M metro, with job growth ranked #3 nationally (+39,700). Demand here would survive any single driver failing.

Hotel Occupancy Collapse

Metro hotel occupancy fell 8.6% in 2025 to 58.9% — the largest decline of any Top-25 US market — against a 62.3% national average. Hotel occupancy is the cleanest unbiased proxy for underlying overnight demand, and it says demand is soft and softening.

World Cup Ceiling Test

Seven FIFA World Cup matches ran at NRG June 12 – July 4, 2026. Hotel occupancy actually dipped 1% to 61%, and STR occupancy reached only 40%. The largest sporting event on earth raised price ($300 ADR, +45%), not volume — that is the ceiling under maximum compression.

Occupancy Is the Structural Weakness

36.1% median occupancy (AirROI), 47% (Chalet), 51–57% (Airbtics/AirDNA/MasterHost). Even the generous reads sit near 50% in a city with year-round business demand — the tell of chronic oversupply, and the opposite of the >60% markets that carry high debt service.

Seasonality — Year-Round, Shallow Swing

No resort-style dead season: every month is bookable and the peak-to-trough swing is shallow (40–45%). Peak March on the Rodeo, soft January–February at ~25% occupancy, plus a heat-and-hurricane block June–September. Sources disagree on which month is the trough, and every 2026 dataset is World Cup–contaminated in June.

Climate Risk Is Severe and Repeating

Harvey (2017, $125B), the May 2024 derecho (~900,000 without power), Hurricane Beryl (July 2024, 2.2M without power, $6B+ damage, week-plus outages) and Winter Storm Uri (2021). Houston recorded three 500-year floods in three years. Two week-plus grid outage events occurred in calendar 2024 alone.

🏠 Competitive & Revenue
Gate Zero — 14.6×

Inner Loop STR-grade median price ~$525,000 ÷ Inner Loop 3BR median revenue $36,000 = 14.6×, a 6.9% implied gross yield. By sub-area: Heights 18.0× (5.6% yield), Montrose 15.3× (6.5%), Museum District/Midtown 12.3× (8.1%), EaDo 10.8× (9.3%, the best in the Inner Loop). Above roughly 6×, a 20% gross floor stops being arithmetically reachable.

The 20% Floor Is Arithmetically Excluded

A 3BR earning $36,000 justifies $180,000 at the 20% bar — $51,000 below the $231,000 1031 minimum. Prices have decisively outpaced revenue: the Inner Loop revenue premium over citywide is roughly +30%, while the price premium is +140%.

Supply +21.2% vs Revenue +5.8%

At 36.1% median occupancy — and Houston has no zoning and no land constraint, so there is no structural supply ceiling at all. Any revenue premium invites immediate, unlimited substitutable competition. A post-ordinance −8.8% supply contraction in Mar–May 2026 is one quarter of tightening, which is not yet a trend.

Not a Small Market

9,033 active Airbnb listings (AirROI), 8,815 (Airbtics), >8,500 registered with the City. All-platform: ~19,355 total records, ~10,000–11,000 genuinely active — far above the 600-listing SMALL MARKET threshold. The flag correctly did not fire.

The Operator Gap Is Real

Only 7.8% of Houston listings are professionally managed and 66.4% run Airbnb’s default Simplified Pricing — the widest operator gap of any market on the board, and the one genuine opportunity here.

Revenue by Bedroom

1BR $20,000 · 2BR $24,907 · 3BR $36,000 · 4BR $40,500 · 5BR $62,039. 5BR is the only band whose 20%-floor justified price ($310,195) clears the $231,000 1031 minimum — and Inner Loop 5BR inventory transacts at $700K–$1.2M, above the search ceiling outright. The sweet spot exists on paper and not on the MLS.

Top Decile $69,684

The citywide top-decile listing earns $69,684/yr, and that revenue is produced by 4–5BR homes costing $700K–$1.2M. Against a $500,000 basis, the 20% floor ($100,000) is 1.43× the top decile and the 30% goal ($150,000) is 2.15× it. Cash flow does not turn positive until roughly $67,000 gross, while the 3BR top quartile is $52,000 — the top quartile does not clear debt.

ADR Growth Is Event-Inflated

ADR is +22.4% YoY, but June 2026 ran a $348 ADR against a $225 market ADR on World Cup compression. Growth is coming from rate, not occupancy — rising revenue-per-listing alongside 36% occupancy and 21% supply growth is a price effect, not a demand effect. Underwrite to de-event-adjusted actuals and discount any seller-provided 2026 revenue.

Best Transactable Lane — EaDo

A 3BR townhome in EaDo (77003) at $340K–$400K is the only Inner Loop pocket with real sub-$500K inventory, and it carries the lowest deed-restriction risk of every area screened. Even here, $43,300 average revenue ÷ $370,000 is 11.7% gross — and $43,300 is a right-skewed average against an EaDo median of $30,000–$35,000.

🔧 Operations & Exit
Property Tax 2.1252% — Uncapped

Summed from the eight actual 2025 HCAD jurisdiction rates (HISD 0.8783 + Harris County 0.38096 + Flood Control 0.04966 + Port 0.0059 + Hospital District 0.18761 + Education Dept 0.004798 + Houston City College 0.098802 + City of Houston 0.51919). No MUD inside the Loop — suburban Harris runs 2.7–3.3%. On a $500,000 buy that is $10,626/yr, reappraised to full market value every year.

Texas Non-Disclosure Advantage — With a Catch

Buying at $500,000 does not automatically reset the HCAD assessment to $500,000, which is a genuine edge and the reverse of Florida. But model property tax off the parcel’s current HCAD market value, not the purchase price: HCAD carries 1325 Dorothy at $625,835 while it is listed at $525,000 — a $2,676/yr swing.

TWIA Does Not Reach the Inner Loop

TWIA covers 14 coastal counties plus only the sliver of Harris County east of Hwy 146, so wind and hail sit inside the standard policy with no separate named-storm deductible — a real, quantifiable advantage over any coastal buy. Insurance $5,700/yr = $4,500 hazard/DP-3 with STR endorsement + $1,200 Zone X preferred-risk NFIP.

Deepest Service Bench Screened

Cleaning runs $80–$165/turn ($140 the working figure for a 3BR Inner Loop unit) with a named redundant vendor bench — River Oaks Cleaning, Regal Rest, Maid U Shine, Cleaning Crew Care — plus full Turno marketplace coverage. Local PM runs 12.5%–18% (HostStarter 12.5% flat, no contracts) against a 20–30% resort norm. Held below a 9 by ~88 turns/yr on short urban stays versus 45–55 in a resort market.

buildingPct Is a Selection Rule, Not a Constant

Six real HCAD 2026 certified parcels returned building shares from 0.075 to 0.734 at similar price points in the same two ZIP codes. The same $500,000 delivers $88,750 or $12,500 of Year-1 bonus deduction — a 7.1× swing — depending entirely on whether the structure or the dirt carries the value. 2101 Lawrence St carries $418,000 of dirt against $34,000 of house; 1636 Vassar St, $460,598 of land against $59,901 of structure.

Foundation Risk — Beaumont Clay

~45% clay content with high shrink-swell. A licensed structural engineer’s report ($500–$900) is a mandatory contingency on every candidate, plus a $6,000 one-time reserve on any pre-2000 structure. A prior repair carrying a transferable lifetime warranty is a positive.

The LTR Floor — Houston’s Distinguishing Feature

Median asking rent for a 3BR Inner Loop townhome (77008, n=35 active) is $3,500/mo, which exceeds the $2,695/mo P&I by ~$805 and recovers ~90% of full PITI ($3,897). A failed Houston STR costs $400–$1,600/mo to hold rather than the full nut — real downside asymmetry that no prior market on the board has offered.

Three Independent Resale Buyer Pools

Owner-occupants (Montrose and the Heights are premier primary-residence neighborhoods, which is why 77008 is +11.6% YoY against a flat metro), builders/developers (a live teardown bid with HCAD carrying Inner Loop dirt at $90–$100/sf), and income investors (99 active rentals in 77008 alone). Offset by unambiguous buyer’s-market conditions: 40,750 active listings — an all-time HAR record — 9.1 months of supply, DOM 52 days, and a 92.2% list-to-sale ratio.

The MTR Lane — The Finding That Outranks the Buy Box

A 30–90 day furnished mid-term rental prices at 1.3–1.6× the LTR rent = $4,500–$5,600/mo, or $54,000–$67,000/yr versus $36,000 nightly. Hotel occupancy tax does not apply at 30+ consecutive nights, so the 17% load disappears, and turnover cost is near zero against ~88 turns/yr. 33.5% of Houston listings already sit at 30+ night minimums — an established segment, not a speculative one.
