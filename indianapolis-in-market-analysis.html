Indianapolis

Indiana — Downtown / Mile Square
Indianapolis is simultaneously the worst median and the best top tier of the ten markets screened in this 1031 search. On the market-wide median the arithmetic is fatal: $28,685 justifies only $143,425 at the 20% floor, roughly $87,575 below the $231,000 1031 purchase floor. But downtown 2BR comps with 55–539 reviews each actually earn $46,500–$79,700, and the model listing earns $171,000. At 7.13/10, Indianapolis ranks above Brevard NC (7.11) and sits almost exactly on the Destin FL anchor (6.9–7.5, Tier C/B border) — exceptional demand and regulation, dragged down by 1–3% condo appreciation, a 27.7% record CBD office vacancy, and a top tier already owned by operators running 18 to 103 units.
Budget Fit — TIGHT
At $365,000 the math requires roughly $80,000 gross to reach a $30,000 net cash flow target — above what all but the top decile of this market produces. The Target lane delivers −$1,692 to +$25,147 on full-cost underwriting. The Aggressive lane needs $149,922 cash against $140,856 available.

⚖ Regulatory & Legal
Permit — Permissive

Revised Code Chapter 852, effective Jan 1 2025: $150/yr per-unit permit from DBNS, applied online via Accela. No owner-occupancy test, no cap, no neighbour notification, no public hearing, no variance requirement. Sec. 852-106(1): short-term rentals are not required to obtain development plan approval. Condos are explicitly named as a permittable structure type.

State Preemption

Indiana HEA 1035 (2018), codified at IC 36-1-24, limits local authority to ban STRs outright. IC 36-1-24-9 allows a city to require a variance for non-owner-occupied STRs — Indianapolis declined to use that power. HEA 1210 (effective July 1 2026) bans local government rental caps and restricts voting on HOA rental restrictions to owners whose unit is their primary residence.

HOA — The Real Risk

State preemption expressly does not reach private covenants. Of six candidate buildings: 450 E Ohio #202 and #114 carry an MLS "Rental Restrictions" disclosure flag (term unknown); 611 N Park Ave #105 indicates a 3-month minimum lease; 355 E Saint Clair shows no flag and markets itself to investors; 488 E 10th is unknown; 735 Elm St appears to have no HOA at all. MIBOR's MLS carries a discrete HOA Disclosures field — presence of "Rental Restrictions" is a hard kill, absence is suggestive but not proof.

Historic Overlay

IHPC holds jurisdiction over Lockerbie Square, Old Northside, Fletcher Place, Fountain Square and Chatham Arch/Mass Ave. A Certificate of Appropriateness is required before a building permit issues for exterior alterations — which directly threatens the glass-garage-door / opening-terrace-wall amenity that inspired this thesis.

Jurisdiction — Resolved

Unigov consolidated Indianapolis with Marion County in 1970 but excluded four already-incorporated municipalities: Beech Grove, Lawrence, Southport and Speedway, which keep their own ordinances inside the county. All four are peripheral; none touches 46202, 46204, 46225, Bates-Hendricks or Garfield Park. Verify per parcel by taxing district on maps.indy.gov — never by mailing address, since all four write their mail as "Indianapolis, IN."

Tax Trap

Redfin and Zillow display roughly $353/mo property tax on these condos — that is a homestead-capped 1% owner-occupant bill. A non-owner-occupied investor falls in Indiana's 2% "other residential" cap class, roughly $663/mo at $365K. Every listing page understates the true carrying cost by about $3,720/yr. Lodging tax is 17.0% combined (7% state + 10% Marion County innkeeper's), auto-remitted by Airbnb/VRBO, exempt at 30+ nights.

📈 Demand & Market Fundamentals
Five Independent Drivers

Conventions, motorsport, pro and college sport, medical, and urban leisure — the deepest demand stack of any market screened in this search. Four are contractually locked past 2028: Gen Con through 2030; FDIC International, the NFL Combine, and the Big Ten Football Championship through 2028. Stress tests for "Colts leave," "Pacers leave," and "conventions soften" all come back survivable.

Scale
  • 207 convention events, 1.8M attendees; Indiana Convention Center + Lucas Oil is one of the largest connected complexes in the US
  • ~70 walkable Colts / Pacers / Fever home dates per year
  • Indy 500 draws ~350,000 — the largest single-day sporting event in the world
  • ~25 million people within a 4-hour drive; IND set a record 10.6M passengers in 2025 across 52 nonstops
Medical — Counter-Cyclical, and Doubling

Riley Children's (475 beds, 23,900+ admissions, 65,200+ ED visits) draws multi-night families from across the state against only 46 Ronald McDonald House rooms. IU Health's new $4.3B, 44-acre, 864-bed downtown campus opens late 2027. This is the leg that fills the non-event calendar.

Hotel Supply Threat

The 803-room, 38-storey Signia by Hilton has NOT opened — construction targets fall 2026 and reservations open Feb 1 2027. A Ritz-Carlton is also under construction, plus three more hotels in development. That is roughly +11% on a ~7,100-room downtown base landing in one quarter, aimed squarely at the convention-headquarters segment an STR competes for.

Event Concentration — Real but Mild

Three of the five busiest downtown hotel nights of 2025 fell inside Gen Con; the single busiest was Indy 500 eve. Roughly 10–20% of annual gross comes from 7–9 nights, with STR ADR ~$365 against ~$189 typical (1.93×). But peak/trough monthly spread is only 1.67× versus 4–6× for a lake or mountain market — this is a year-round market with a spike, not a single-peak market.

🏠 Competitive & Revenue
The Top Tier Is Two Companies

The model listing "Penthouse of all Penthouses" ($171K, $573 ADR, 90% occupancy) belongs to Austin, who runs 103 listings with three co-hosts and 7,027 lifetime reviews. His listing text states: "We own other units in the building if you want to book multiple." He also owns the $80K and $73K listings. The $109K unit is CozySuites, a multi-city aparthotel brand occupying purpose-built corporate-housing stock at The Block — not for-sale inventory. Four of the five top listings are two operators. Zero are solo owner-operators.

But the Formula Is Learnable

"Indy Urban Nest" ($75K) belongs to Anh, who built an 18-listing operation in two years — not ten. Her formula sits in plain sight in her own titles: "Skywalk" appears in 6 of 10 and "Arcade" in 6 of 10. Skywalk connection + arcade/game amenity + sleeps-many is the local winning play, and it is amenity-based rather than scale-based. This is the single most encouraging fact in the report for a new entrant.

The Feature Is Not the Moat

An Airbnb search of downtown returned four other rooftop/terrace penthouses on page one alone — 5.0 (55 reviews), 4.95 (93), 4.85 (52), 4.85 (202) — plus more at 3–4BR. Live Redfin inventory surfaced two more rooftop units that were never on the board. Outdoor space downtown is common, not scarce. What is scarce is the combination plus the operating platform behind it.

Supply Is Contracting in the Target Segment

2BR/2BA listings are down 9.0% downtown and 7.5% citywide year-over-year, against +4.3% revenue growth. The +21.8% figure reported by AirROI is all-bedroom — the supply boom is in 3BR+ houses in the neighbourhoods (38.4% of listings), not downtown 2BR condos. The investor's target segment is the one segment shedding supply. REVENUE_TREND: FLAT-TO-GROWING.

Calibration & the Two-Source Failure

AirDNA reads roughly RIGHT here — the Houston direction. AirDNA citywide 2BR/2BA $24,345 vs AirROI all-listings $22,782 is 6.4% apart, no meaningful bias. But never blend their ADR/occupancy pairs (AirROI $228 at 36.5%; AirDNA $121.66 at 59% — different denominators). ⚠ The downtown 2BR band rests on AirDNA ALONE — AirROI publishes no bedroom-level revenue for Indianapolis, Chalet 404s. Tagged Confidence: Low — single source, mitigated by the fact that the comp table is actual trailing revenue for nine named listings carrying 55–539 reviews each, not a modelled band.

Sweet Spot — 2BR Confirmed

2BR is the dominant configuration at 25.4% of all Indianapolis listings, it is the segment shedding supply, and every one of the nine credible downtown comps is a 2BR. This is one of the few markets screened where the standing 2BR/2BA preference and the data agree rather than conflict. Maximise capacity inside the 2BR footprint (bunks, sleeps 8–10) rather than buying a third bedroom.

🔧 Operations & Exit
Appreciation — The Worst Finding

Downtown condos compound at 1–3%/yr from actual recorded sales against 2026 assessed value. 450 E Ohio #318 sold for $309,500 in 2018; its 2026 AV is $308,500 — $1,000 BELOW, eight years later. Three of four sampled condos fell or stalled 2025→2026 while a townhome rose +7.1% and a Bates-Hendricks house +9.2%. ZIP 46204 ZHVI +1.9% against the city's +3.9%, in a state whose median rose +52% 2019–2024. CBD office vacancy is 27.7% — a record high (Colliers Q1 2026).

No LTR Floor

A downtown 2BR rents for about $1,950/mo against a $3,265/mo carry (P&I $1,967 + tax $663 + HOA $535 + insurance $100). Misses by $1,315/mo — $15,780/yr, an LTR DSCR of 0.60. Property tax and HOA alone consume $1,198 of the $1,950. If the STR thesis fails there is no soft landing. Resale is also soft: condo DOM 65 (up 40 YoY), sales −25.2%, 50.5% of listings cutting price.

Self-Management Is a Requirement

A PM-managed unit at the comp median falls $4,223/yr short of the mortgage alone. PM-managed opExpRatio is 0.628 at $52,100 and 0.512 at $75,000. There is no version of this deal that survives third-party management.

Cost Seg — the Premise Refuted

Marion County Assessor, 2026, from real parcels: downtown high-rise condo 0.885 (n=282), townhome 0.867 (n=3), Bates-Hendricks SFH 0.888 (n=355). The expected condo cost-seg advantage does not exist — the spread is under 2 points and runs the wrong way against the SFH. The high building share is cheap Midwest land, not the condo form. ⚠ Verify per building: 319 E 16th sits at 0.735.

Ecosystem Depth

50 pages of property managers; the top 10 alone control 909 properties; seven named STR cleaning vendors; 15.1% of listings professionally managed and 55% Superhosts. Among the deepest operating environments of any market screened — though depth does not rescue the math. Minor friction: high-rise turn logistics, and recurring elevator complaints appear in competitor reviews.

Cost Inputs (verified)
  • Property tax 2.18% of market value (2% cap class + referendum levies, which sit outside the cap)
  • HOA condo: $440 / $535 / $800 per month ($5,280 / $6,420 / $9,600 a year); townhome/PUD $197–$265
  • HO-6 walls-in with STR use: $900 / $1,300 / $1,900; townhome full dwelling $1,600 / $2,300 / $3,200
  • A townhome does not beat a condo on dues — the $3,720 headline gap nets to about $30 once you add back roof/HVAC reserve, higher dwelling insurance, and unbundled utilities. Its real argument is control and resale liquidity.
  • Indiana quirk: a separate 1–2% wind/hail deductible, which on a condo lands on the HOA master policy and converts into special-assessment risk — buy loss-assessment coverage.
