The 20% floor and 30% goal are not properties this market sells; they are prices it does not produce.

At 6.21/10, Hocking Hills ranks in the lower-middle tier among cabin and nature-anchored STR markets — narrowly above comparable state-park cabin markets like Luray, VA (6.00, Tier D — run Aug 23 2026). Aggregate demand demonstrably sustained (county lodging tax more than doubled 2019→2024), but per-listing economics did not: supply grew 35–50% into a slower-growing pie while prices compounded 5–9%/yr.
Budget Fit — TIGHT
At $492,500, hitting $22,000 of net cash flow needs about $87,500 gross — above this lane’s $75,000 ceiling, and about $107,800 once the mandatory remote-owner costs (cleaning + local contact) are counted. The market’s own pricing law says $492,500 buys roughly $61,500 of gross.
⚠ Revenue Trend — Declining
Five independent negative reads on revenue-per-listing: Logan −8.2% · South Bloomingville −19.7% · Rockbridge −5.4% · Laurelville −2.6% · AirDNA −1.8%. Underwrite to recent actuals, and discount seller-provided revenue from peak years by 20–30%. One listing here advertises $111,000 (2023) and delivered $44,000 (2025).
⚠ Jurisdiction Warning
“Hocking Hills” is a tourism brand, not a jurisdiction — it spans five counties. Unincorporated Hocking County has no zoning at all, but City of Logan caps STR licenses at 60 and bans them in R-1; Berne Twp (Fairfield/Sugar Grove) requires a discretionary CUP with a 5%-of-township cap; and Hide-A-Way Hills bans sub-31-day rentals in writing — grandfathering does NOT transfer to a buyer. Confirm the governing county by parcel, never by mailing ZIP: one carry-forward property mailing to “Logan 43138” is actually in Perry County.

⚖ Regulatory & Legal
No Zoning At All

Unincorporated Hocking County has NO zoning, and no Hocking County township has adopted zoning (Good Hope, Laurel, Benton, Falls, Marion, Ward, Salt Creek, Green, Perry, Starr, Washington). No owner-occupancy rule anywhere in the market. Out-of-state ownership is explicitly accommodated. Enforcement today is, per County Regional Planner Audie Wykle, “the wild, wild west.”

Draft Ordinance Pending

Hocking County STR Ordinance Draft 6 (Jan 29 2026) was recommended to the Commissioners Feb 12 2026 and remains unadopted. Terms are workable, not hostile: $50/yr permit, ~$350 pass-through inspection, no cap, no owner-occupancy, 18-hour minimum stay, $1M liability, BCI check, 60-minute local contact, 1-year + 6-month compliance runway. The real teeth are county driveway/emergency-access standards and a landscaping buffer within 200 ft of an adjoining parcel.

Hide-A-Way Hills — Dead Zone

Code of Regulations Art. II §4(F)(2) (amended 8/18/2024): no rental or lease under 31 days. Deed restrictions separately require any lessee to be an accepted Club member. The 2005 settlement grandfathering does NOT transfer — “future owners of these properties are generally subject to the above stated prohibition.” You cannot buy a currently-operating HAH short-term rental and continue it.

City of Logan — Restrictive

Ord. 54-2023 §157.163: STRs prohibited in R-1; hard cap of 60 active licenses in R-2/R-2-B; 500-ft spacing; max 3 per owner; $50 application + $500 annual; max 10 guests; renewals accepted only Oct 1–Dec 1. License transferability on sale is undefined in the code — a real turnkey risk.

Berne Township (Fairfield / Sugar Grove)

Effective Feb 2026: conditional-use permit from the BZA, 2-acre minimum, shared driveways prohibited, 1,000-ft separation, 24-hour minimum stay, renter 21+, emergency contact within 30 minutes, annual renewal with “NO GUARANTEE OF RENEWAL,” and a hard cap at 5% of township houses.

Ohio Taxes

Lodging tax 6% unincorporated (3% county + 3% township) / 10% in Logan. Neither Airbnb nor Vrbo remits Ohio local lodging tax — monthly owner filings, 10% late penalty rising to 25%. Conveyance fee $4 per $1,000 (seller pays) in Hocking/Fairfield/Perry/Vinton; $1 in Ross. CAUV recoupment claws back 3 years of savings on conversion.

SB 104 — Do Not Underwrite On It

Ohio’s pro-STR preemption bill (7.5% lodging cap, bars local bans/caps/owner-occupancy mandates, one-time $50 license) remains “As Introduced” in Senate Local Government Committee as of Aug 21 2026. It has passed neither chamber.

📈 Demand & Market Fundamentals
Columbus at 52 Minutes

Columbus MSA 2,242,028 — the 15th-fastest-growing large US metro, growing at double the national rate — sits 43 miles / 52 minutes away. ~12.5M people within 3 hours, ~21M within 4. This is the single fact that makes midweek sellable here, and it is unmatched among comparable cabin markets.

Demand Is Verifiably Growing

County lodging-tax receipts $1,311,640 (2019) → $2,971,738 (2024), +127%. A Tourism Economics study released Aug 20 2026 puts total economic impact at $295M (+9.1%), 1,635 jobs, 14.1% of county employment. The anchor is permanent ODNR public land — free admission, no gate, no concession that can lapse.

Flattest Season Of Ten Cabin Markets

AirROI ranks Hocking Hills the flattest revenue curve of ten US cabin markets at 2.6–2.7× peak-to-trough. August is the top month (12.6%) — October is the co-peak (12.4%): fall is a price event ($384 ADR), summer a volume event. No single week exceeds ~4% of annual revenue. February is the only true dead month (4.8%); Q1 still carries 16.6%.

Revenue Floor — Passes Thinly

February modeled at $2,320 against the $2,006 requirement (70% of $2,865.66 monthly debt service) — +15.7% headroom, 81% of full debt service. The pass is hot-tub-dependent: AirROI measures a +242.6% revenue uplift for a hot tub here ($59,413 with vs $17,340 without). A cabin without one fails the floor outright.

Weekend Concentration — And How The Top Tier Beats It

The median listing books 42.1 reservations × 2.4 nights = ~101 nights/year against ~104 Friday/Saturday nights — the median cabin is literally a weekend-only business. Top-decile operators at 73–77% occupancy sell ~84 more nights, and those can only come from Sunday–Thursday. Midweek capture IS the value-add thesis, and Columbus is what makes it possible.

Forward Catalyst

Ohio’s first mountain coaster (3,640 ft) opens spring 2027 in Logan — an all-weather winter/rainy-day anchor aimed squarely at the market’s weakest season. The 2022 State Park Lodge (81 rooms) is a demand generator: its conference center manufactures midweek group demand.

Two Claimed Drivers Struck

Dark Sky is REFUTED — John Glenn Astronomy Park is not IDA-certified (Ohio’s certified sites are Observatory Park and Fry Family Park). Do not put “Dark Sky Park” in a listing title. Intel/New Albany has slipped to 2030–32 and must not be underwritten; its only near-term effect is tight trades labor inflating rehab budgets.

🏠 Competitive & Revenue
The Governing Law — 8× Gross Revenue

Hocking Hills prices short-term rentals at ≈8× gross annual revenue (5.5×–11.9× across 14 disclosing listings). $500,000 buys ≈$62,500 of gross — a 12.5% yield. The market clears at 12–13% at every rung. Buying $100,000 of gross costs ~$800,000; $150,000 costs ~$1.2M.

Three Disclosed T-12 Actuals (Browser-Verified)

$70,000 at 21221 Sterling Rd (asking $599,000 — 8.6×) · $56,909 FY2025 at 10104 Frasure-Helber Rd (asking $575,000 — 10.1×) · $120,000 at Stargazer (asking $979,000 — 8.2×). The first two are the best-presented no-moat 2BR/2BAs on the market: professionally managed, hot-tubbed, on private acreage — and they gross 1.3× the median.

Corrected Benchmarks

Median gross $48,250 (AirDNA) vs $48,360 (AirROI) — 0.2% apart, high confidence. But two carry-forward figures are corrected: working ADR is $360–$410, not $259; and top-decile occupancy is 73–77%, not 94–98% (the higher figure is scraped-calendar data counting owner blocks). Market-average occupancy is only 40–42% at a 2.4-night ALOS.

Supply — The Defining Weakness

AirROI sub-market growth: Logan +35.5% · Rockbridge +50.9% · Laurelville +48.7% · South Bloomingville +48.1% against five independent negative reads on revenue-per-listing. Corroborated by Commissioner Michael Linton: “we have more cabins than we have demand for cabins.” Source conflict flagged: AirDNA reports only +6.6% — a 5–8× disagreement absorbed into the confidence rating, not the score.

The Moat Thesis — Proven

Stargazer (observatory + stocked pond) grew $115K→$120K, +4.3%, while the un-moated field fell 1.8–19.7% and 25755 Amerine Rd collapsed $111K→$44K (−60%). Within one operator on one parcel, Dunlap Hollow’s conventional cottage asks $250/night while its cave asks $820–$1,200 — 3.3× for the structure alone.

But Not Every Distinctive Structure Earns A Premium

“Steel Nest” is a genuine container home, turnkey, hot-tubbed, on 4.72 acres at $485,000 — and its live nightly rate is $240. Even at top-decile occupancy that is ~$65,760. Test a moat on its live nightly rate, not on the adjective. If the rate is not ≥2× median ADR, the moat is decorative and the property earns median-tier revenue.

Sub-Area Surprise

Proximity to Old Man’s Cave does NOT drive ADR. South Bloomingville is closest (~10 min) but has the lowest ADR ($314) because 40.3% of its stock is 1BR micro-cabins. Rockbridge is ~22 min out and commands the premium ($410 ADR, $52,044 median, $130,668 top-decile) because 52.5% is 3BR+. ADR tracks bedroom count, not drive time. Proximity buys occupancy, not rate.

Amenity Ranking

(1) On-parcel natural feature 2–4× ADR · (2) distinctive structure 2–3.3× · (3) pool +187% at only 6.9% prevalence · (4) hot tub = table stakes at 93–96% prevalence — its absence costs 50–70% of revenue · (5) sauna/wellness stack = the cheapest tier-jump available to a 2BR buyer · (6) 3-channel distribution. EV charger showed no measurable premium — skip it.

🔧 Operations & Exit
Ohio Property Tax — Worse Than A Reset

Post-sale rate is 1.47% of price = $7,350/yr, against sellers currently paying just 0.33–0.34% of sale price — +$5,694/yr, or 9% of gross revenue. Core district = 43.012 effective Class I mills. The Hocking Auditor has ALREADY reclassified STRs to be valued at STR fair-market value, “resulting in higher values.” H.B. 186 (Dec 2025) phases the 10% non-business credit to zero by TY2029 — the STR loses both credits, not just the 2.5%. 2025 was Hocking’s sexennial reappraisal; next update TY2028.

Operating Costs — Rebuilt From Local Vendors

miscSupplies = $18,400 — the standard $4,000 baseline is off by 4.6×. Itemized: propane, electric, septic pumping + aerator contract, well treatment, hot-tub service and chemicals, winter freeze protection, gravel drive regrading and snow/ice removal on steep hill drives, tree and limb work, pest/humidity, trash haul, Starlink in a low-signal hollow, and generator/outage provisions. Insurance MID $3,400.

Property Management Is Non-Viable Here

Hocking Hills full-service PM runs 40–50% of gross, not the standard 20–25% (1st Choice 50% full / 25% booking-only; Hocking Hills Management 40–50%). At 45% the expense ratio reaches 0.909. A Florida-based remote owner must run co-host + independent cleaner: local contact $3,600/yr, cleaning $6,720 (42 turns) to $18,240 (114 turns) at $160/turn. This market bills by the turn, not the night, at a 2.4-night ALOS.

Cleaning Ecosystem — Genuinely Abundant

10+ named local cleaning and turnover operators, a CVB vendor directory, and one-stop vendors bundling hot-tub service with cleaning. Wage pressure moderate ($14–15/hr). This is the opposite of the Arizona findings — the on-site-contact problem is priced, not a blocker. Note the draft ordinance would make a 60-minute local contact a legal requirement.

Septic Caps Your Revenue

Ohio sizes HSTS at 120 gpd per bedroom (OAC 3701-29-11), so a 2BR system caps the listing at ~6 guests permanently absent a $15,000–$25,000 replacement. Hocking County does not require a transfer inspection — which is exactly why the $205 county property inspection with water test is the highest-value line in pre-offer diligence. Watch for shared wells: one verified listing discloses one.

Ohio FAIR Plan Is NOT A Backstop

Contrary to the working assumption, the Ohio FAIR Plan’s homeowners program covers owner-occupied primary residences only — not seasonal or investor STRs. An out-of-state STR gets at best a bare dwelling-fire form: no business liability, no loss of rents. Price real perils: severe convective storm, hail, wind/tornado, and winter freeze/burst pipe (a major claim category in a part-time-occupied hot-tub cabin).

Exit — The Worst Category

Median household income $62,960 supports a ~$190K–$250K purchase, exactly where healthy activity sits (63-day pending, 96.9% of list). Above ~$450,000 there is ZERO owner-occupier participation — 100% investor pool, all running the same negative math. Evidence: 417 DOM through 11 cuts unsold · five failed listings since 2021 including a $300,000/33.3% cut in 16 days · 319 DOM · 448 DOM with a fall-through · three failed campaigns since 2023.

LTR Fallback Does Not Cover Debt

Realistic 2BR long-term rent of $1,000–$1,600/mo ($12,000–$19,200/yr) against $34,388 of annual debt service — a $15,000–$22,000 shortfall. Even an outlier-high $1,791 Zestimate leaves a $12,896 gap. Resale is the only real exit. Appreciation underwritten at 3.0%/yr — county-wide prints of 7.6–9.2% are dominated by sub-$300K owner-occupied stock the $450K+ STR segment is not participating in. County population is shrinking, −6.5% since 2010.
