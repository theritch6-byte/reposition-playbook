STR Market Analysis · September 6, 2026

Red River Gorge

Kentucky
Red River Gorge scores 5.20/10 — Tier F, Pass. At 5.20/10, Red River Gorge ranks at the bottom of the mountain/cabin STR markets screened — below Hocking Hills, OH (6.21), its closest structural analogue, and marginally below Eureka Springs, AR (5.21) — making it the second-lowest of the eleven markets screened to date, above only Williams, AZ (4.98).

The shape of the failure is the finding. Demand is genuinely strong (8/10) and regulation is permissive (7/10) — five independent drivers, ~500,000 annual RRG visitors on the US Forest Service’s own count, a 1.9M–4.3M two-hour drive ring, and unincorporated Powell County with no STR ordinance and no zoning ordinance at all. Peer-reviewed EKU research refutes the fragile-single-driver worry outright: climbers are ~1.5% of visitors, and Wolfe County’s third-best revenue month is June, not a climbing month.

Everything between that demand and a return fails. Wolfe County supply grew +60.9% in twelve months (~46 → 74 listings) while per-unit revenue fell −16.2%. The top revenue tier is owned by two professional platforms — The Canopy Crew (11 units) and RRG Getaways (32 units) — with zero solo owner-operators in it. Appreciation is measured negative on six independent instruments, not merely flat. And the exit is not slow but broken: the corridor’s flagship STR cabin has been marketed for thirty-one months and a 10.5% price cut without selling, while Slade has recorded two sales, both in H1 2024, and nothing since.

Nothing in this report is a recommendation, and no property named in it belongs on any buy list. Every listing shown is market-structure evidence that inventory exists at a given price band — nothing more.

⚑ Revenue Trend Caution — Declining
Per-unit revenue is falling across all seven measured geographies, on two independent data houses, over the trailing twelve months to July 2026: Wolfe County −16.2% · Slade −15.0% · Pine Ridge −9.5% · Campton −8.9% · Rogers −8.8% · Stanton −6.3% · Powell County −4.1%. ADR rose while occupancy fell — the textbook oversupply signature, not a demand story.
⚖ Jurisdiction Warning — Four Taxing Counties
The corridor spans four taxing counties and is mostly unincorporated — unincorporated Wolfe is 0.913% property tax with a 0% local insurance premium tax, while Beattyville is 1.603% and Stanton/Clay City carry a 10% insurance premium tax; a $3,421/yr swing on $485K that is pure jurisdiction choice. Avoid City of Stanton limits entirely: Ord. 22-002 requires an annually-reviewed, NON-TRANSFERABLE conditional-use permit, so you do not inherit the seller’s permission. Confirm governance by taxing district, never by mailing address.

⚖ Regulatory & Risk Intelligence
Hensley v. Gadd — the real Kentucky kill-switch

560 S.W.3d 516 (Ky., Nov. 15, 2018). The Kentucky Supreme Court held that running a property as a short-term rental is a commercial use that violates a deed restriction limiting lots to single family residential purposes — even though the house looks residential — because nightly, weekend and weekly occupants were not residents. It binds statewide and reaches raw unincorporated land with zero zoning; it needs no HOA, no board and no enforcement budget, because any single neighbouring lot owner can enforce it. Kentucky sits on the minority side of the national split, so an out-of-state investor’s general instinct here is wrong.

  • A per-property Schedule B title read plus a county clerk restrictions search is mandatory, not optional.
  • A metes-and-bounds parcel outside a platted subdivision is worth real money over a subdivision lot at the same price.
  • Covenant prevalence by subdivision is a documented data gap — covenants are recorded at the county clerks and not indexed online.
No ordinance, no zoning, no preemption

Unincorporated Powell County’s complete published ordinance index — current through Ordinance 2025-08 — contains no STR ordinance and no zoning ordinance, and the county has no planning, building-inspection or code-enforcement department at all. Unincorporated Wolfe is looser still. No Kentucky statewide preemption exists either: SB 110 (2025) would have barred local CUP requirements and density caps and did not pass; SB 9 (2026) died on the final day with STR preemption reported as the sticking point. Direction of travel at state level is pro-STR pressure that has now failed twice — a future win would be upside, not downside.

City of Stanton — Ord. 22-002, the hardest jurisdiction

Inside Stanton limits an STR needs a Conditional Use Permit through the Board of Adjustment, reviewed annually; is allowed only in R-1 and R-2; needs a non-transferable STR business licence at $300 first unit / $100 each additional; must provide off-street parking with no on-street guest parking; may display no sign advertising the property as a rental; and must admit the Zoning Administrator into the interior on notice. An annually-renewed CUP is discretionary permission rather than a vested right, and a non-transferable licence means you do not buy the seller’s permission — the same trap seen in Eureka Springs, AR and Houston, TX. Lodging tax stacks to ≈13.4% inside the city against ≈10.2% in unincorporated Powell.

The jurisdiction cost map — $3,421/yr of pure choice

Total real-estate rates computed line-by-line from the KY DOR 2025 Property Tax Rate Book, not an aggregator. Kentucky assesses 100% of fair cash value uniformly with no investor class (homestead is 65+/disabled only), so the real trap is staleness: rural PVA assessments run far below market, and a cabin showing a $600 bill will carry ~$4,400 once revalued toward a $486,933 sale. Underwrite at 0.898–0.913% of purchase price, never at the displayed bill.

Landslide is an uninsured peril — and it hits the top revenue tier

Standard homeowners policies exclude earth movement, NFIP covers flood only, and the Kentucky Mine Subsidence Insurance Fund expressly excludes landslide. Powell County took repeat 2025 mudslides including the Mountain Parkway Exit 33 (Slade) slide that damaged eight vehicles; Powell, Wolfe and Lee were all FEMA IA+PA designated under DR-4864-KY. The cliff-perched cabin earning $413–$611 ADR carries an uninsured total-loss tail that this market’s own photography actively sells. Pull the Kentucky Geological Survey landslide inventory (kgs.uky.edu/kgsmap) on any sloped candidate, and pull the parcel-level FEMA zone from msc.fema.gov by address rather than accepting a listing agent’s zone claim.

Septic, building code and the Wild & Scenic corridor

Kentucky septic is 110 gpd per bedroom (902 KAR 10:085, Table 1) — more permissive than the 120–150 in comparable markets, and the 2–4 guest product only needs 1–2 bedrooms, so septic is not the binding constraint. The KY Dept. of Housing, Buildings & Construction lists Local Building Inspector: None for Powell, Wolfe, Menifee and Lee, and health-department environmentalists hold walk-in permitting hours one morning a week. Separately, the Red River through the Gorge is a National Wild and Scenic River — a 19.4-mile designated segment, Kentucky’s only one — so riverfront inside that corridor is a federally-managed reach rather than ordinary riverfront. Its CRMP corridor width and private-land restrictions are unread and remain a blocking gap on any riverfront candidate.

📈 Demand & Market Fundamentals
The smoking gun — supply outran demand roughly 4-to-1

Wolfe County — the heart of the corridor — went from ~46 to 74 active listings in twelve months, +60.9%, while revenue fell −16.2%. Powell added +12.9% to 35 listings against −4.1% revenue. Supply-weighted corridor supply growth is ≈ +45.5% across 109 listings; supply-weighted revenue ≈ −12.3% YoY. Price is holding while volume collapses: Wolfe ADR is $254, higher than Powell’s $220, yet Wolfe occupancy is 34.5%, lower than Powell’s 39.6%. New cabins are being priced up and are simply not filling. The loss is occupancy, not rate — the textbook signature of oversupply rather than demand loss.

Five independent drivers — and the fragile one is not the revenue base

(1) Natural Bridge State Resort Park, 300–400K/yr with its own lodge, skylift and trails; (2) Daniel Boone NF / RRGGA hiking and 100+ natural arches, ~500K/yr; (3) sport climbing; (4) Nada Tunnel and the scenic drive; (5) Red River kayaking and canoeing plus zipline and underground-kayak operators. Peer-reviewed EKU research (Maples, Clark, Sharp et al.) puts RRG at ~7,500 unique climbers a year — roughly 1.5% of the ~500K visitors. Climbers are also the wrong customer: 44% earn under $30K, many are full-time students, and the study’s own six-county lodging sector supported six jobs. Climbers camp; they do not book $220/night cabins. Wolfe County’s third-best revenue month is June, not a spring climbing month — direct evidence that family hiking, water recreation and Natural Bridge carry the core.

The bullish narrative is a paid developer press release — and its timeline is false

The circulating one-million-visitors / supply-hasn’t-kept-up claim was issued by Ian Teal, Developer at Highlands of Red River Gorge, distributed by BusinessWire and explicitly labelled a paid press release, citing no primary source. The real figure is ~500,000/yr per USFS Cumberland District program manager Eric Dodd. The ~1M traces to a 2017 WYMT interview in which a park naturalist summed Natural Bridge SRP (300–400K) with RRGGA (500K+) — so the claim that visits passed one million during the pandemic and have grown since is false by three years against its own source. And supply has not kept up is the exact inverse of the measured truth. The recruitment campaign is itself part of the mechanism depressing the returns it advertises.

Drive ring, market type and the missing secondary layer

Verified 2025 MSA populations: Lexington 535,174 (~1 hr) · Louisville 1,402,509 (~2 hr) · Cincinnati 2,312,858 (~2–2.5 hr) · Columbus 2,242,028 (~3.25 hr) · Knoxville ~807,000 (~3 hr). The ~2-hour ring holds 1.9M–4.3M people, the ~4-hour ring 7.3M+. With no meaningful commercial airport this is a 100% drive market — a durability asset (insulated from airfare shocks) and a ceiling liability (no national demand pool). AirROI classifies Powell County a micro-market; the corridor is 109 active listings, late-majority / saturating rather than an early-mover window. Secondary demand is essentially absent — no corporate, medical, university or relocation base across 67,647 people in 1,431 sq mi, so there is no weekday floor and no shoulder-season fallback.

Seasonality quantified — three dead months, not four

Peak-to-trough is 3.20× (Powell) / 3.16× (Wolfe) — roughly 2× worse than a 1.67× urban comp but below the 4–6× lake/ski band. Powell peaks in October at $4,899 / 64.6% occ / $244 ADR and troughs in February at $1,530 / 28.6%; Wolfe peaks October at $5,203 / 54.0% / $283 and troughs January at $1,649 / 20.7% — about six nights a month. Powell’s peak grouping is October/March/April (the climbing windows); Wolfe’s is October/November/June. Residual risk is October concentration: one bad October does outsized damage.

Forward signal — new access fees are a headwind

Daniel Boone NF proposed day-use fees at 22 sites, 19 of them in the Gorge: $5/day, $7/3-day, $50/yr where trailheads are currently free, with backcountry camping rising $5 → $10. The stated rationale was a dramatic increase in visitation. That adds friction to the free-hiking driver that carries most of the volume. Meanwhile low regulatory barriers cut against the owner: they are precisely why Wolfe County supply grew +60.9%. There is no regulatory moat protecting an incumbent’s revenue, and the loosest county is the one building fastest.

🏠 Competitive & Revenue Intelligence
Gate Zero — price-to-revenue ≈ 8.6× to 9.4×

Red River Gorge listings average $312,152 across 112 for-sale listings against Rogers’ $36,436 average revenue → 8.6×. AirDNA’s own best-mountain-towns pairing for Rogers ($459,500 median list / $49,000 potential) → 9.4×. Two independent routes, the same answer. Above ~6× the 20% floor is arithmetically unreachable — this market fails the multiple test before any property is examined. Against the $51,900 1BR corridor median the buyer’s ceiling reads 9.38×.

Operator concentration is the headline

8 of the 9 visible top comps belong to just two professional platforms. The Canopy Crew LLC (11 properties) owns #1, #2, #3, #4, #6 and #9; RRG Getaways (up to 32 Airbnb listings, 100+ cabins managed) owns #5 and #7. Zero solo owner-operators in the top tier. The two conventional cabins clearing $70K — Honeydew ($89.7K / $282 ADR / 87% occ) and Date Night ($79.1K / $282 / 77%) — are the same operator, sister units in the four-cabin Shawnee Run cluster alongside Sweet Rock and Sugar Maple. A guest shut out of one is funnelled to a sibling, and that portfolio-capture effect is structurally unavailable to a single-cabin owner. The decisive number: RRG Getaways’ 32 listings gross $1,025,923 — an average of $32,060 per listing, and roughly $28,600 once the two flagships are stripped out.

Measured revenue tiers — the basis for all three lanes
  • Tier 1 (top 10%) — $82,752–$115,700 · $413–$611 ADR · 46–87% occ. Needs a purpose-built irreplaceable structure or a multi-cabin cluster. Canopy Crew + RRG Getaways only.
  • Tier 2 (top 25%) — $56,640–$85,128 · $318–$319 ADR · 53–60% occ. Hot tub + view + real design + 2-night minimum + Superhost ops. Reachable — and where a buyer must land to survive.
  • Tier 3 (median) — $27,148–$42,000 · $179–$247 ADR · 36–41% occ. Everything else, including the listings parked on 30+ night minimums.
  • Bottom quartile — $14,316–$23,172 · $132–$194 ADR · 21–26% occ. No hot tub, weak photos, or effectively dormant.
The median is an artifact — and it does not rescue the market

Between 32.8% (Rogers) and 45.5% (Pine Ridge) of active listings carry a 30+ night minimum in a market whose average stay is 2.3–2.7 nights. Roughly four in ten competitors cannot accept the weekend booking that is this market’s demand unit, yet they sit in the denominator dragging median occupancy to ~37%. So the real competitive bar is higher than the median suggests, and Honeydew’s 87% is not an anomaly — it sits above Rogers’ 76% top-decile occupancy threshold. This corrects the read on achievable occupancy but changes nothing about the verdict, because both routes to the top are gated: the ADR lane needs a purpose-built structure, and the occupancy lane needs multi-unit overflow capture plus 125+ remote self-managed turnovers a year.

Moat test — measured multiples against the $214 market ADR
  • Suspended / bolted cliff house — 2.85× (Sandstone Drifter), 2.42× (Cliff Dweller). Real and the largest premium, but a built moat, not a bought one.
  • Pirate-ship architecture — 1.87×–1.95×. Real; same builder, same exclusion.
  • Mirrored treehouse — 2.32× ADR but only 46% occupancy. An ADR moat that does not convert to nights.
  • Domes — 1.23× ADR, 50% occ, $48.4K. Weak: unique ≠ premium, consistent with the 0.87× dome finding elsewhere on this board.
  • Clifftop view + sauna + hot tub on a conventional cabin — 1.32×. Modest; the money here is occupancy, not rate.
  • Hot tub — +197.3% revenue uplift ($34,080 with vs $11,465 without, AirROI Powell). Table stakes. Its absence is a penalty; its presence is not a differentiator.
  • River frontage — no isolable premium. The Gorge’s draw is the cliff line, not the river, and riverfront carries the flood premium. The moat and the hazard are the same parcel.

The cliff pays the builder, not the buyer.

Data calibration — AirDNA reads HIGH here, and the error scales with radius

Powell County: AirDNA $30,651 / 41.0% vs AirROI $28,265 / 39.6% — +8.4%, nearly identical. Slade: AirDNA ~$45,700 / 59% vs AirROI $27,486 / 37.7% — +66%. This inverts the Broken Bow calibration and matches the Luray direction, so every revenue tier used in this report is AirROI-sourced. ADR is two-source corroborated; occupancy is not — AirROI reads ~37–41% where AirDNA and Airbtics read ~57–59%, and the two were not blended. Dual-platform listing is the market standard: Honeydew is live on both Airbnb and Vrbo, and AirDNA review counts exceed live Airbnb counts, consistent with cross-platform merging.

🔧 Operations, Appreciation & Exit
Appreciation is measured negative, not merely weak

Every corridor parcel pulled is cutting price and none has sold: 637 Cave Hollow $419K → $375K over 31 months unsold; 507 Cave Hollow −7.1%; 67 Pine Mountain −11.5%; 198 Lacy Ln −12% in 27 days. Powell County $143/sqft, −7.4% YoY; a held parcel’s Zestimate reads −16% in one year. Wolfe County median sale $348K, −11.5%; Powell $199K, −6.1%, with days-on-market blown out from 79 to 206. appreciationRate = 0.00 is the charitable assumption, not a conservative one — the honest stress case is −5%/yr. One contrary datapoint, stated honestly: Redfin puts 40380 Stanton at $219,851, +7.2% YoY on a small sample against county- and parcel-level instruments that are all negative.

The exit is not slow — it has stopped

Slade’s sold index shows two sales, both in H1 2024, and nothing since. Powell County sold 6 homes in July 2026, −37.1% YoY. The buyer pool for a $325–400K Wolfe County cabin is STR investors and only STR investors — not local owner-occupants (Wolfe median income $16,967, county median home $110–186K), not second-home retirees at scale, not institutions — and that buyer must underwrite the same falling-revenue, rising-supply, 9.38× market, which by the parcel evidence they are currently declining to do at any price sellers will accept.

The LTR floor fails by ~$2,000/month

Rent Zestimate $1,136/mo against a $3,258/mo carry at budgetMid (P&I $2,625 + tax $367 + insurance $267) = DSCR 0.35 — roughly twice as bad as the previous worst market on this board (0.60). It still fails at a $300,000 purchase (carry ~$2,026 vs $1,136 rent = 0.56). This is structural rather than negotiable: the price is set by STR income expectations while the rent is set by Wolfe County wages. There is no mid-term lane either — no corporate, medical, university or contractor base in a six-county area of 67,647 people. The only exits are sell, into a market where the flagship cabin sat 31 months, or feed it.

The cost-segregation consolation prize — read it against the exit

buildingPct measures 0.876 for a newer build on ~1 acre — above the IRS newer-build benchmark band of 0.75–0.82 — because Appalachian land is genuinely cheap ($2,581–$8,867/acre in bulk). On a $486,933 purchase that is a ~$426,553 depreciable basis, roughly $37,000 of extra basis created purely by cheap dirt, and self-management is the strongest fact pattern for the material-participation test. The figure is robust to a price cut (0.839 at a realistic $280,000 clearing price) and to property type (0.749 for a mid-age build on 7.68 acres). But cost segregation is a timing benefit, not a return — it accelerates deductions the buyer would receive anyway, and it is recaptured on sale. In a corridor where the flagship cabin has sat 31 months unsold, the recapture event is indefinitely deferred because you cannot get out. A large depreciable basis on an asset you cannot sell is a consolation prize, not a thesis.

Operating cost stack — derived, not benchmarked

grossRevRef = $51,900, the credible 1BR corridor median from a saved AirDNA comp pull; the budget-derived fallback of $73,040 ran 41% high and was correctly rejected. opExpRatio_reference = $13,366 / $51,900 = 0.258 — propTax $4,409 (0.9055% Powell/Wolfe blend) + insurance $3,400 + miscSupplies $4,000 + platform $1,557. No PM or co-host fee appears in any base case — the buyer self-manages. The PM reference (0.478) and the published 20% local co-host rate are carried as context only and were never modelled. Insurance adds $1,400–$4,000/yr NFIP flood on any riverside or hollow-bottom parcel, which alone would move opExpRatio from 0.258 to 0.335. Lodging tax is 10.0% in unincorporated Powell (under the 12% flag) but 13.0% inside Stanton city, and Powell’s 3% is a separate manual monthly county filing — budget $500–$1,500/yr of compliance.

Cleaning ecosystem — limited, and the constraint is bodies

Real operators exist — Natural Bridge Cabin Company (est. 2001), Red River Gorge Cabin Rentals, Scenic Cabin Rentals, Red River Stays — and labour is cheap, so this is not a cold start. But Wolfe County has 6,298 people and the highest unemployment in Kentucky (11.1%, Feb 2026) against +12.9% to +60.9% supply growth, and every operator in a violently weekend- and October-peaked market needs the same cleaners on the same Sunday. High unemployment in a 6,000-person county co-existing with a growing fleet is a structural mismatch, not absorbable slack. The per-turn rate was NOT obtained and is an open data gap — the $75–$125 band is inferred from county wage data, not observed. Market cleaning fee averages $84 (median $75), charged by 100% of listings, ≈12.7% of revenue and guest-reimbursed; the pro formas carry only the net $16/turnover.

Unresolved source conflict — logged, not silently resolved

637 Cave Hollow Rd. operations.md reports it as a cabin marketed since Feb 2024 (31 months), cut $419,000 → $375,000, unsold. buybox.md reports the same address as a $45,000 vacant 0.95-acre LOT (MLS 24002597), and notes that none of the three Cave Hollow addresses appears in Redfin’s live 41301 set. A prior working file recorded 637 as a $399,000 treehouse cabin, 750 sqft. These cannot all be true — most likely multiple parcels share the road number, or the listing was relisted or re-parcelled between pulls. Resolve by Wolfe County PVA parcel ID before any offer. The appreciation and exit conclusions do not depend on this parcel and stand on independent evidence.
