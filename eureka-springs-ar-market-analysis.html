STR Market Analysis · August 23, 2026

Eureka Springs

Carroll County, Arkansas
Eureka Springs scores 5.21/10 — Tier F (Pass). The demand is real and durable: an entire National-Register Victorian city that cannot be reproduced, a year-round haunted-tourism economy, a Great Passion Play that drew 112,000+ in 2025 on its best per-show attendance in eighteen years, and a Northwest Arkansas metro of 622,177 — the 9th-fastest-growing in the country — 45 to 60 minutes away. Nothing here argues that people will stop coming.

The arithmetic is the story, and the arithmetic fails. Against a consensus market-median gross of roughly $25,000, a $500,000 purchase is a 20.0× price-to-revenue multiple — the worst of the nine markets screened in this series, on a scale where 5.0× is the 20% floor. Operating costs at the median equal or exceed revenue before debt service (opExpRatio 0.949 self-manage, 1.027 remote). Supply is arriving at 30–36%/yr into demand growing ~9%/yr, and the February revenue floor fails at both the $500,000 and $300,000 bases with no mid-term-rental fallback.

The structural finding that ends it: the median Eureka Springs property justifies $125,000 at the 20% floor, while the 1031 exchange requires a purchase of at least $231,000. The median is not marginal here — it is arithmetically excluded. The 30% goal is unreachable at any compliant price: a top-decile $61,700 producer supports only $205,667.

One live exception is on the board and is not dismissed: 190 N Main St, $329,000–$345,000, 3bd/3ba, projecting $69.2K at HIGH comp strength = 4.75×–4.99× at ask, on a commercial lot where tourist lodging is by right. But it rests on a projection rather than a disclosure, and ‘can be utilized as nightly rental’ is potential, not operation — so it likely fails the 1031 already-producing test. At 5.21/10 this ranks at the bottom of the mature drive-to heritage markets screened, below Luray VA (~7.0) and far below Banner Elk / Shenandoah Valley (7.6–8.4).
Budget Fit — TIGHT
At $300,000 in unincorporated Carroll County, hitting your $22,000 net cash flow target requires about $67,600 in gross revenue — well above the ~$49,300 this lane's best real listing projects, and nearly 3× the market's $23,000 median. Only the by-right commercial downtown lane comes close, and only at its ceiling.
⚠ Revenue Trend — Declining
Per-listing revenue is falling. City lodging-tax receipts rose 9.0% while supply grew ~36%, implying roughly −20% per listing — corroborated by AirROI at −16.9% (Holiday Island) and −16.7% (Berryville). Underwrite to recent actuals, and discount any seller-provided revenue from peak years by 20–30%.
⚠ Jurisdiction Warning
Ordinance 2311 bans new short-term rentals in Eureka Springs' R-1/R-2/R-3 residential zones, and the conditional use permit is expressly non-transferable on sale. Buy only in C-1/C-2/C-3 commercial parcels (tourist lodging by right) or in unincorporated Carroll County (no zoning at all). Confirm the parcel's zoning in writing with the city before any offer.

⚖ Regulatory & Legal
Permit Is NOT Transferable

Eureka Springs Title 14, verbatim: "The conditional use is granted to the applicant for a specific use at a specific property and is not transferable." A new owner must win a fresh noticed public hearing and can be denied. The B&B sale matrix confirms it: "If the B&B is sold, the B&B requires a new CUP." For a 1031 buyer, this is the difference between buying a business and buying a building.

New STRs Banned in Residential Zones

Ordinance 2311 (Oct 2021) bans new short-term rentals in R-1/R-2/R-3, enforced at $250/day. A 200-ft spacing rule separates lodging uses in R-1 Victorian. Only Title 14 Appendix A's closed 18-address list ("No additions may be made to this list", pre-9/30/1986, no 180-day operating lapse) survives a sale. Roughly 20 of the 43 listings in the $230–520K band sit in this category and are legally unusable.

Two Clean Lanes Do Exist

Tourist lodging is permitted BY RIGHT in C-1, C-2 and C-3 — no conditional use permit, therefore no transferability exposure at closing. And unincorporated Carroll County has no zoning and no STR ordinance at all. These two categories are the entire buyable universe here.

Corps Docks Cannot Be Rented — "There Are No Exceptions"

36 CFR 327.30(d)(4) and the 2018 Beaver Lake Shoreline Management Plan prohibit including a private Corps dock in any rental agreement, with penalties to $5,000/6 months and summary revocation. New Beaver Lake dock permits have been closed since late 2020. This matters doubly: it kills the only moat that cleared the 2× ADR bar, and it means any "private dock" STR comp in this market is operating non-compliantly.

Tax Load Over the Flag — and Owner-Remitted

Combined city tax load ≈14.375%, above the 12% warning threshold (unincorporated county ≈9.0%). Airbnb auto-remits the 3% CAPC tax and Vrbo only since 6/1/2025 — every other channel, including direct bookings, is owner-remitted. Title 14 also requires a CAPC good-standing letter to continue a permit, so a delinquent seller can void the buyer's lodging rights.

Hearings Land Inside the 1031 Window

City Council meets Monday, Sept 28 2026 — the exact identification deadline (2nd/4th Monday cadence). The last Planning Commission before the 02/10/2027 close is Tuesday Feb 9 2027, one day out. Separately, Arkansas SB 197 would preempt Ordinance 2311 — the in-city supply moat is legislative and repealable in a single session.

Holiday Island Is Excluded

The Holiday Island Suburban Improvement District's STR permit is expressly non-transferable AND revocable at will. That is not a lane; it is a liability.

Documented Data Gaps

Every fee, renewal cycle, inspection requirement and occupancy/minimum-stay rule — for both Eureka Springs and Holiday Island — is unpublished, as is whether the B&B conditional-use moratorium (last extended to 8/15/2024) is still live. These need City Hall calls (479-253-9703 / 479-379-8040) before 09/28.

📈 Demand & Market Fundamentals
Demand Is Durable and Multi-Anchor

The entire city is NRHP-listed Victorian (built 1880–1910; Arkansas' 2nd-largest city in 1889, now ~2,000 people) — physically unreproducible supply of the thing people come for. No single point of failure across the anchor set.

The Great Passion Play Is GROWING, Not Fading

112,000+ attendance in 2025 — the best per-show attendance in 18 years, with the mortgage retired in 2023. The "fading attraction" hypothesis this run went in with is refuted.

The NWA Feeder Is Real and Durable

Northwest Arkansas: 622,177 people (Jul 2025), +2.4%, +14,744/yr — the 9th-fastest-growing US metro, +41% since 2010, and only 45–60 minutes away. XNA airport carries 2.52M passengers (+10.4%), now larger than Little Rock, with a $109M concourse underway. This is what structurally separates Eureka from a purely rural market.

Mountain Biking: Eureka Is a Node, Not a Spillover Catcher

Eureka Springs is a listed OZ Trails node ("Bella Vista to Eureka Springs") with Walton-funded trails inside the city, plus Lake Leatherwood — 1,600 acres, 25 mi cross-country, a 7-line downhill gravity zone with a paid shuttle. The framing that it merely catches drips from Bentonville is wrong. Dollar magnitude remains unquantified.

Other Verified Anchors
  • Thorncrown Chapel — 250+ weddings/yr; ~8M visitors over 45 years
  • Beaver Lake — ~3.1M visitors/yr (Confidence Medium; developed recreation concentrates on the Benton County end)
  • Compression stack: Bikes Blues & BBQ (275–300K, Rogers, 45 min) + fall foliage + Halloween/paranormal makes October the peak of peaks; Diversity Weekends compress April
Durable Floor, Low Ceiling — Supply Has Passed Demand

Occupancy at 33.6% settles the maturity question. Demand is durable but demand growth is capped, while the unincorporated ring adds supply at 31–36%/yr into flat demand. The market has also split: city supply is legally frozen (~24 grandfathered) while the ring grows — which is what reconciles city A&P receipts +9% against platform supply +36%. Different geographies, not contradictory data.

Two Live Negatives

AGFC emergency trout limits on the Beaver Dam tailwater after a hatchery crash — the fishing segment is currently suppressed, which undercuts any White River frontage thesis. Tyson cancelled 39 Carroll County grower contracts, hitting both the local economy and the Berryville/Green Forest long-term-rental fallback.

Data Gap

No visitor-origin data exists — the drive-vs-fly split (~85–90% drive) is inference, not sourced. More actionable gap: city-limits vs unincorporated listing counts, which would size the capped-supply moat directly.

🏠 Competitive & Revenue
The Signature Is OVERSUPPLY — Confirmed by a Non-Scraped Source

ADR is flat (+0.1% — no pricing power) while occupancy sits at 29–34% across four sub-areas. The clincher is municipal, not scraped: CAPC lodging-tax receipts +9.0% (first 8 months 2025, $1.38M vs $1.26M) against supply +36% ⇒ ≈−20% revenue per listing. AirROI independently measures −16.9% (Holiday Island) and −16.7% (Berryville). Prediction matches measurement — this is not a geofence artifact.

Supply: AirDNA Is the Outlier for the 5th Straight Market

AirDNA headline +5.7% vs AirROI +36.2% (and +31.0% on a second window). AirDNA's own 3-year chart says ~630 → 1,004 listings ≈ +59%/3yr, contradicting its own headline. Satellites: Holiday Island +47.7%, Berryville +76.5%. Working figure: +30–36%/yr. The pipeline is entirely outside the city, because Ordinance 2311 froze the core.

Revenue Floor FAILS at Both Price Bases

February median listing $1,154/mo. Against full monthly debt at $500K ($2,865.67) that is 40.3% — far under the 70% bar. At $300K ($1,719/mo) it is 67.1% — still under. There is no mid-term-rental fallback (30+ night minimums are 0.1% of supply), so this is binding, not academic.

Only One Moat Clears 2× ADR — and It Is Illegal to Rent

Beaver Lake shoreline + dock + large hot tub tests 2.21–2.46× — and Corps rules forbid renting the dock. Everything else fails: downtown walkability 0.74–1.03×, hot tub alone 1.09×, White River frontage 0.69–0.87×, valley-view-plus-hot-tub 0.87× (the same decorative multiple a dome tested at in a prior market).

The $90K+ 2BR Tier Is Real but 3–6 Listings Deep

~2–4% of the 2BR slice. Confirmed arithmetically — Lake Shore Cabins' 2BR publishes $445/night → $109K at top-decile occupancy. But of the three named screen comps, "Beaver Lakefront Cabins" is a 7-cabin couples RESORT, not a buyable 2BR, and the other two could not be located by name. Calibration also marks the tier down ~12% to ≈$79–82K.

The Bedroom Ladder Barely Pays

Measured at only ~1.10–1.14× per bedroom off two published rate cards. This is a couples market where the second bedroom adds little — which is exactly why 1BR is 46–57% of supply. 2BR bands: median $22K–$31K, top quartile $35K–$53K (2 sources, 2 methods, Confidence Medium, deliberately wide).

Dual-Platform Is the Market Standard

Multi-channel 59.2% · Airbnb-only 25.5% · VRBO-only 7.8% · Booking-only 7.4%. Every pro forma here assumes dual-platform listing; single-platform operators typically achieve 15–20% less gross. Hot tub penetration is only 22% — a differentiator by scarcity even though it fails the 2× ADR test on its own.

Small-Market Flag Does NOT Fire · Data Gap

All-platform count 835 (AirROI) / 1,004 (AirDNA) — both well above the 600 threshold, so Supply confidence is not capped on that basis. Data gap: no free bedroom-level revenue table exists for this market — Chalet returned 429 four times, Rabbu 403, all three AirDNA public pages 403, Awning is login-walled, Airbtics has no Eureka page, and PriceLabs is not enabled on the account.

🔧 Operations & Exit
Operating Costs Meet or Exceed Revenue at the Median

The single most damning operating fact in this analysis: opExpRatio = $21,822 / $23,000 = 0.949 self-manage, 1.027 for a remote owner, and 1.169 with a property manager. At the market median, operating costs consume or exceed gross revenue before a dollar of debt service. The corresponding market-median cap rate is 0.38%.

Amendment 79's Cap RESETS on Sale

Arkansas assesses at 20% of market value. No limitation may be claimed until the second assessment date after transfer, and the 10% non-homestead cap shelters only later growth, not the reset. Carroll County reappraised +34.21% since 2022 and a 2026 buyer eats it all at once, with no homestead credit for a non-resident. Effective rate 1.004% of purchase price → year-2 tax $3,012 at $300K, $5,020 at $500K.

miscSupplies = $15,220, Not $4,000

Rebuilt from named local vendors (range $8,800–$27,360), including a $1,400 steep-gravel-drive line — steep drives are a confirmed Eureka problem and already cost one board property its place — and a $600 historic-district upkeep line for in-city Victorian stock held to Historic District Commission standards.

Insurance: 1–2% Wind/Hail Deductible, and No FAIR Plan

This is tornado and hail country. The deductible norm is 1–2% of Coverage A — $3,500–$7,000 out of pocket on a $350K dwelling — and Arkansas has NO FAIR Plan backstop. STR-endorsed premiums: $1,900 low / $2,900 mid / $4,800+ for historic downtown stock carrying age surcharges.

Septic Caps the Guest Count — and Karst Can Kill a Deal

Arkansas sizes by bedroom count, not a published gallons-per-day figure (the flow table sits in ADH Appendix B, offline): 1,000-gallon tank for 1–3BR, 95 ft of trench per bedroom. Guest cap: 2BR = 4 guests, 3BR = 6. Eureka sits on springs and karst limestone — percolation failure is a genuine deal-killer and aerobic units are the norm on Ozark ridges.

Cost Segregation Is the Strongest Line in the Model

buildingPct from real assessor parcels (actDataScout, primary data — no benchmark used): downtown Victorian 0.78 (237 N Main .793, 217 N Main .804, 55 Vine .743); Holiday Island mid-age 0.89; larger downtown 0.94. At 100% bonus depreciation (OBBBA 2025) on the 25% reclassified share at a 37% bracket, Year 1 cost seg returns $19,841–$24,315 depending on lane — more than any lane's operating cash flow. Data gap: the rural-acreage segment could not be pulled.

The LTR Fallback Does NOT Cover Debt

At $250K, long-term rent clears gross by $2,006 but leaves a $7,480/yr net shortfall. At $300K it fails on gross outright. Eureka is not the second market in this series to clear that bar — and the Tyson contract cancellations weaken the Berryville/Green Forest workforce demand the fallback would rely on.

Exit: Thin, and Negotiation Room Is Narrow

Sale-to-list ≈95.0%, 53 days on market, ~24 sales/month county-wide. That 95% is decisive for the buy box: any lane needing more than a ~5–8% discount is not realistically negotiable. Median county sale $310K = 13.5× price-to-revenue at the $23,000 median.
