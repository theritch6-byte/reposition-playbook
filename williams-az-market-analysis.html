STR Market Analysis · August 21, 2026

Williams

Arizona · Coconino County
Williams scores 4.98 / 10 — Tier F (Pass). It is a real market with a permanent, world-class demand anchor and one of the strongest regulatory shields in the United States — and it still does not work, because the price of entry has decoupled from what the properties earn. Assessor values rose 40–50% cumulatively from 2023 to 2026 while STR revenue fell 8–24% over the same window, a roughly 50-point divergence that has compressed the conservative cap rate to about 2.0% against a 7.75% note. That is 5.5–6.5 points of negative leverage, and it is the whole story: a median 3BR at $400,000 runs about −$2,600/yr, a median 2BR runs −$8,200/yr, and at the full $484,000 ceiling the deal loses $24,922/yr against a +$22,000 target — a ~$47,000 miss. Seven of the framework's ten red flags trigger.

At 4.98/10, Williams ranks at the bottom of the scale among national-park-gateway drive markets — well below comparable gateway markets like Luray, VA (6.00, Tier D — run Aug 23 2026) and Shenandoah Valley, VA (7.6–8.4, Tier B), and below even the framework's designated low anchor for a single-driver market that never sustained (5.4–6.2). Shenandoah runs a 21% average gross ROI on a $450–$750K budget; Williams runs 7.4% at its median in-town sale.
Budget Fit — TIGHT
At $390,000, hitting $22,000 net cash flow requires about $66,300 in gross revenue — roughly 50% above the $44,078 top of the 3BR band in Williams. That figure still excludes the legally mandatory local responder and the cleaning shortfall; loading those in pushes the requirement to about $76,900, against a market whose top-decile performer of any size earns $79,584.
⚠ Revenue Trend — Declining
This market has shown declining STR revenue across five independent sources and two consecutive periods (AirDNA −7.0% then −8.0%; RevPAR −11.5% then −11.3%; Chalet active-operator revenue −23.8%). Underwrite to recent actuals, apply conservative growth assumptions, and discount seller-provided revenue from peak years by 20–30%. 2026 is also the Route 66 centennial — haircut a further 5–8% for 2027 forward.
⚠ Jurisdiction Warning
City of Williams and unincorporated Coconino County are two different STR regimes. Inside the city, Ch. 117 requires an emergency contact on-site within one hour plus a sex-offender check on every guest of every booking, and Ordinance 1000 blocks new sewer taps and ADUs through 7/1/2027. In the county, STR permits are issued only for permitted habitable dwellings — no yurts, domes, park models, or RVs. Confirm which jurisdiction governs the parcel before making any offer.

⚖ Regulatory & Legal
State Preemption

A.R.S. §9-500.39 / §11-269.17 are statutory state preemption: an Arizona city may not prohibit vacation rentals or short-term rentals, may not impose owner-occupancy, and may not cap or space them. Courts keep reinforcing it (Oak Creek v. Sedona, Ct. App. 2025). This is the single strongest thing about Williams as a market.

Permit Cost & Speed

Williams Ch. 117 / Ord. 995: $190 year one, $140 per renewal — the cheapest in the region — with a 7-business-day issue-or-deny guarantee and no cap. Neighbor notification is required; neighbor consent is not, and there is no lottery. Permits are non-transferable, so a buyer applies fresh after closing rather than inheriting the seller's.

One-Hour On-Site Rule

§117.04 requires the designated emergency contact to be physically on the premises within one hour when summoned by police or fire; the City's registration form tightens this to a 30-minute phone response and 60-minute on-site arrival. §117.10 requires a sex-offender background check on every guest of every booking, retained 12 months and producible to police. Operating from Florida, pure self-management is not legally available here — a local co-host is a licensing condition, not an optimization.

Political Direction of Travel

Mayor Don Dent testified to the legislature seeking a 10%-of-housing-stock cap, citing ~15% of Williams' ~1,500 units lost to STRs and Section 8 vouchers falling 50 → 17. HB2429 passed the AZ House in March 2026 and died in the Senate with its sponsor recommitted to reintroduction; SB1076 would let towns under 70,000 cap permits — Williams at ~3,200 qualifies by 20×. The League of Arizona Cities made STR restriction a 2026 priority explicitly targeting investor-owned STRs. Preemption currently rests on Senate leadership discretion, so permitting early likely earns grandfathering.

Building Moratorium

Ordinance 1000 is confirmed live through July 1, 2027 — verified against Council minutes through 8/13/2026, including a unanimous 7/23/2026 denial of an exception for a 672 sq ft Habitat for Humanity home. Any ADU, casita, or new-sewer-tap strategy inside city limits is dead. It also freezes competing supply, which cuts in a buyer's favor.

Water, Fire & Insurability

Williams ran 17 ppb arsenic against a 10 ppb EPA limit through 2025 and took its last groundwater well offline on 1/23/2026 — the city now runs entirely on surface water. County modeling shows a post-wildfire monsoon putting downtown under up to six feet of floodwater and debris. Arizona has no FAIR plan, so insurance must be bound, not estimated, during due diligence, and a standalone flood policy is mandatory.

CC&R Risk

Formal HOA density is low, but A.R.S. §9-500.39 binds governments, not associations — a pre-existing recorded CC&R banning STRs is fully enforceable and preemption-proof. Kalway v. Calabria Ranch (Ariz. 2022) protects existing rights against retroactive amendment but does nothing about a restriction already on record. Clear CC&Rs per-parcel at the Coconino County Recorder before the inspection deadline.

📈 Demand & Market Fundamentals
Independence Test — Failed

Only one genuinely independent demand driver exists: the Polar Express (Nov 7 – Dec 30, ~8 weeks, wholly controlled by Xanterra), plus one partial in Bearizona. The Grand Canyon Railway, Route 66 traffic, and the town's entire 42-hotel stock are all downstream of the national park. Every other night sold in Williams is a Grand Canyon night.

The Anchor Itself

Grand Canyon South Rim drew 4,430,653 recreation visits in 2025 — a permanent, world-class draw. Guest origin is genuinely diversified: the top five feeder cities account for only 17.8% of bookings. Phoenix is ~3 hours, Las Vegas ~4, Los Angeles ~7 — a clean drive market on I-40.

Visitation Fell, Revenue Followed

Park visits dropped ~10% in 2025 (4.9M → 4.43M, Dragon Bravo Fire plus a 3% system-wide dip), moving the Canyon from #3 to #4 nationally. Williams STR revenue moved in step (−4.3% to −8.0%), confirming the transmission is direct and unbuffered.

No Off-Season Floor

Secondary demand is effectively zero. The BNSF crew-lodging hypothesis fails verification — the crew-change points are Winslow and Needles, not Williams. Travel-nurse demand belongs to Flagstaff Medical Center 35 miles east. The Railway, Under Canvas, and NPS all house their own seasonal staff. The only real off-season prospect is construction crews from the $33M SR 64 project.

December Is the Peak, Not July

The Polar Express makes December the highest-revenue month ($4,014 gross, $245 ADR) — larger than July. It is a genuine second peak with zero January tail: the run ends Dec 30 and spring break is not until March, leaving a clean two-month hole. February is the verified floor at $2,072 gross, 31.1% occupancy, $190 ADR.

Forward Construction Risk

The $33M SR 64 reconstruction runs spring 2026 through fall 2027 — 18 months of delay on the exact road every guest drives to reach the canyon.

🏠 Competitive & Revenue
Small Market Flag — Triggered

AirDNA prints 836 all-platform listings, but that boundary sweeps the whole gateway corridor (Williams + Parks + Garland Prairie + Valle + Tusayan + Ash Fork). Four sources put Williams-city Airbnb supply at 213–313, and StaySTRA says 92.2% of listings touch Airbnb, so deduped city-proper all-platform supply is ~340–400. Below the 600 threshold — treat every median as directional, not precise, and Supply confidence is capped at Medium.

Revenue Trend: Declining and Accelerating

AirDNA −7.0% (Jun) then −8.0% (Jul) YoY revenue, RevPAR −11.5% then −11.3%. AirROI −4.3%. StaySTRA −3.4%. Chalet Q2-2026 active-operator revenue −23.8% while occupancy rose 9.1 points — the signature of a price war, not a supply glut.

Supply Is Flat — The Decline Is Demand-Side

Supply is −0.6% YoY all-platform (AirDNA) / +3% (Rabbu). An earlier +43.6% figure was AirROI's Airbnb-only listing-count change — a dataset-coverage artifact, corrected here. This matters: price wars do not resolve by waiting for absorption the way supply gluts do.

2–3BR Is the Most Crowded Shelf

1BR + 2BR are 62.7% of all supply — the intake's target slice is the single most contested segment, while the top decile is populated almost entirely by 4–6BR group homes. The market's #1 listing at $164,375/yr is Sleeps 16 | Jacuzzi | Escape Room | Polar Express.

The Gap Is Amenity, Not Design

Presentation is mature — 33.7 photos per listing, 56.2% Superhost, 42.8% Guest Favorite, 114.6 average reviews. But hot tub penetration is 11%, pool table 4.2%, game console 3.8%, only 27.2% professionally managed, and ~48% are Airbnb-only. A hot tub, a game room, dual-platform listing and dynamic pricing is a genuine, cheap edge — it is simply not enough to overcome the basis problem.

Booked Nights vs Available Nights

The 25%-vs-48% occupancy conflict across sources is real and explainable: ~37 bookings × ~2.5-night stays ≈ 92 booked nights ≈ 25% of the calendar, versus 42–48% of available nights. Underwrite on booked nights. At ~37 turns against a $75 median cleaning fee, cleaning is a net cost centre.

🔧 Operations & Exit
Property Tax Is Cheaper Than Expected

Arizona's Prop 117 means Limited Property Value does not reset on sale, and every levy applies to LPV — verified exactly on parcel R0004829. LPV runs a median 49.4% of Full Cash Value. Real Class-4 investor bills against actual sale prices came in at 0.402% / 0.321% / 0.258%; the pro formas use 0.45% conservatively to absorb the 5%/yr escalator and Rule-B reset risk.

The Elevation Tax

Misc supplies come in at $9,500, not the $4,000 baseline — itemised as $1,900 propane, $900 on-demand snow plowing, $500 freeze protection, and the rest. That $5,500 premium is what 6,770 feet costs, and it lands on every lane regardless of revenue.

No Williams-Based Manager Exists

Every operator identified — Evolve, Gathering Places, Lost Resort PM, R2 Hospitality, PMI Northern Arizona, Dwell, Delight Homes — is Flagstaff-based, 35 miles / ~40 minutes via I-40. That is at the edge of the one-hour on-site obligation on a clear day, on a road that closes for snow in exactly the months that generate frozen-pipe and no-heat calls. This is the highest-priority unresolved operational risk and must be solved before an offer, not after.

Cleaning Runs a Structural Loss

37 turns × $165 = $6,105 paid vs $2,775 collected = −$3,330/yr, 45% recovery — and the market cannot absorb a cost-recovering fee at a $190–$220 ADR. The labour pool is thin: ~340–400 listings in a town of ~3,200 (drifting to ~2,900) where Grand Canyon Railway & Hotel alone employs ~300 FTE plus ~100 seasonal at hotel scale with schedule stability.

Land-to-Building From Real Parcels

Pulled from Coconino County Assessor EagleWeb (TY2026) across 14 real Williams parcels, seven of which sold Feb 2025 – Jun 2026: small in-town lot 0.87, mid-age standard lot 0.77, Sherwood Forest 0.70, remote acreage 0.90. Pro-forma value 0.80. The driver is land value per parcel, not age or view — which means cost-seg benefit is inversely correlated with STR-viable location here.

Cost Seg vs the Licence — A Direct Conflict

The legally required local responder's hours count against the owner under the material-participation test's more than any other individual standard. Separately, on a 1031 the depreciable basis is the carryover basis from 204/206, not the purchase price, so cost seg cleanly reclassifies only the excess basis — materially less than a headline bonus-depreciation figure implies. Both need a CPA-designed plan before closing or the losses are trapped as passive.

No LTR Floor

A Williams 3BR rents unfurnished at a realistic $1,800–$2,200/mo. At $2,000/mo on a $400,000 basis, NOI lands near $9,760 against $27,509 of debt service — DSCR 0.35. There is no mid-term or travel-nurse floor either; that demand sits in Flagstaff. If STR revenue keeps sliding there is no floor under this asset other than selling it.

Compression and Exit

Assessor FCV rose +40% to +50% cumulatively 2023→2026 while STR revenue fell 8–24% — a ~50-point divergence in three years. Gross yield at the median in-town sale is 7.4% and the cap rate ~2.0%. On the way out: 13.3 months of supply, DOM 109 and rising, closings −25% YoY, median sale −13.5% YoY, sale-to-list 94%. The buyer pool is 100% discretionary money — the same cohort watching revenue fall.
